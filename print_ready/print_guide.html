Fan V1 · Bambu Lab P2S · PETG

Print package — ready for Bambu Studio

Three optimized blades, 12 identical copies each, assembled into three fans and judged by a blinded feel test against the flat baseline. The STLs are pre-oriented on-edge, watertight, single-body, in millimetres — import and they land correctly on the plate.

Fit confirmed. The blade is 227 mm long; the Bambu P2S build volume is 256 × 256 × 256 mm (verified), so it fits with ~15 mm to spare. Note: there is no “P2C” model — the P2-series printer is the P2S (the P2S Combo just adds the AMS, irrelevant for single-material PETG). The STL filenames still read P2C_blade_* — harmless, they are the correct files.

2 Bambu Studio — step by step

  1. New project → select your P2S printer → Generic PETG (or Bambu PETG Basic) filament.
  2. Import a P2C_blade_*.stl. It arrives lying flat, already seated on the plate. Leave it, or right-click → Auto orient to let Bambu pick the lowest-support angle.
  3. Right-click → Set number of instances → 4, then Auto arrange. Four fit per plate (see §4).
  4. Apply the settings in §3.
  5. Slice → sanity-check the preview (brim present, supports only light under the edge undulations, bore is a clean tunnel) → export to SD / send to printer.
  6. Repeat per design and per plate (3 plates × 12 blades ÷ 4).

3 Settings (PETG on P2S)

Filament
PETG. Nozzle 250–260 °C, bed 70–80 °C (start from the Bambu PETG Basic profile).
Build plate
Textured PEI plate — PETG bonds hard; the smooth/Cool plate can chip. On a smooth plate, use a glue stick as a release layer (not for adhesion — to protect the plate).
Layer height
0.16 mm (0.20 mm ok/faster). Finer resolves the on-edge sidewalls and the Ø3 bore.
Orientation
Lying flat — baked into the STL, resting stably on the bed. It arches ~37 mm (the camber), so it prints with supports. Or use Bambu Auto-orient for the lowest-support angle.
Brim
Required. Outer brim, ~5 mm (5 loops). The blade rests on a thin curved edge — the brim is its stable base.
Supports
Required — turn them ON. Tree / organic, top Z-gap ~0.2–0.25 mm (PETG fuses to its supports). A curved shell needs support under the arch in any orientation — normal, not a defect.
Walls / infill
3–4 wall loops + ~15–20% gyroid infill. The STL is solid, so infill builds the internal truss (stiffness) and keeps mass down. Do not print 100% solid (~490 g/fan, over budget); ~20% infill ≈ ~250 g/fan.
Cooling
PETG fan ~40–60% (Bambu profile default is fine). If the enclosed chamber runs hot and strings appear, crack the door.
Nozzle
Stock 0.4 mm brass is fine — PETG isn't abrasive, no hardened nozzle needed.

4 Plate plan

Per plate
4 blades (227 mm along X, four across Y with ~10 mm gaps + brims)
Per design
3 plates × 4 = 12 blades
All three
9 plates total for 36 blades
Filament
~30–40 g/blade incl. supports → ~0.4–0.5 kg PETG per fan, ~1.3–1.5 kg for all three. Buy 2 spools. (Slicer gives the exact number.)
Time
long — plan for multiple multi-hour plates. Slice one to get the real estimate.

5 Bill of materials (per fan)

Pin
Ø3 mm steel or brass rod, length per §1 (57–60 mm). The bore is modeled at Ø3 mm; PETG may print it slightly under — ream to 3 mm or use a Ø2.9 mm rod for a light sliding fit.
Retainers
a cap / washer / push-nut each end with light axial preload — friction then holds the fan at any open angle (the V1 hold; no click).
Blades
12 identical prints of the chosen design.

6 Assembly

  • Peel brims/supports; deburr the bore ends and the edge where the brim was.
  • Thread 12 blades onto the pin, all the same way up (camber the same direction) in a clean z-stack — the bosses form the column, the blades fan out by rotation.
  • Cap both ends with light preload; check it opens/closes and holds by friction.

7 ⚠ Before you commit 36 blades

Print ONE blade first — confirm the on-edge brim/supports peel clean and the tip surface is acceptable (it carries the marching-cubes texture; the slicer/ironing usually handles it). Then print one pair + pin and check the fold nests and deploys. Design 00 especially — it's only 0.5 mm under the 90 mm fold cap, so verify 12 actually stack before committing its full run. Then print the remaining plates.

8 Known limitations (V1, by design)

  • Printed solid + infill, not the raw TO shell. The TO hollowed the core so hard (74–86% void mid-span, paper-thin skins) that the hollow STL was marginal to print and looked broken edge-on. Filling it solid + ~20% infill makes it printable, robust, and stiffer. Proper fix is V1.5: re-run the TO with a stiffness objective + buckling constraint so it grows real internal trusses.
  • Design 00 is marginal on stress (FOS ~1.9, elastic — won't break in a feel test) and fold (0.5 mm). In for its top wind.
  • Un-optimized CAD hub root (the TO clamped a retired radius); sound for V1, fixed in V1.5.
  • No click detent — friction pin gives a firm hold instead.
